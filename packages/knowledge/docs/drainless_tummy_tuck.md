# Drainless Tummy Tuck

I developed and published the progressive tension technique that makes
this possible. This is not a trend. It is over a decade of refinement —
in the OR and in the literature.

## What is a drainless tummy tuck

Most tummy tucks use surgical drains because the procedure creates a
large potential space between tissue layers — and fluid fills that space.
My approach eliminates the space itself.

Using progressive tension sutures, I secure the abdominal flap directly
to the underlying fascia at multiple points across the abdominal wall.
This distributes mechanical load, closes dead space, and removes the
conditions that make drains necessary in the first place.

The result: no external drains, less tension on the incision, earlier
upright posture, improved scar quality, and a more comfortable recovery
— supported by over a decade of published clinical data.

## Why the technique matters, not just the name

Many surgeons now advertise "drainless" tummy tucks. The label is not the
differentiator. The execution is.

Dead space must be structurally eliminated — not just managed. When
progressive tension sutures are placed correctly across the abdominal
wall, fluid has nowhere to accumulate and drains are genuinely
unnecessary. When the technique is approximated, the seroma risk remains
and the drain is simply removed earlier.

The difference shows in the data: traditional drain-based abdominoplasty
has seroma rates up to ~9%; properly executed progressive tension
technique brings it to approximately ~2%. Published across 12+ years of
experience and over a thousand patients.

## Candidacy

- Loose or overhanging abdominal skin following pregnancy or weight loss
- Muscle separation (diastasis recti) and weakened core support
- Loss of natural abdominal contours
- Discomfort or instability related to abdominal wall laxity
- Scarring or tension problems from prior drain-based surgery

This is not a procedure for someone seeking a minor refinement. It is
structural reconstruction of the abdominal wall — appropriate for
patients who have completed childbearing, have meaningful laxity, and
want a result that is both natural and durable.

## Outcomes

My goal is not artificial tightness. It is an abdomen that looks
natural, moves naturally, and stays that way. When this technique is
performed correctly, the abdomen looks athletic — not over-pulled.

## Muscle repair: restoring the foundation

Pregnancy and significant weight changes frequently cause diastasis
recti — a separation of the midline abdominal muscles that affects core
stability, posture, and appearance. Skin excision alone does not fix
this.

Precise midline repair is a core component of every abdominoplasty I
perform. This is biomechanical restoration: restoring the structural
foundation of the torso, not just changing its surface appearance.

## The painless part is real — here's why

"Painless" is not a marketing word in my practice. It reflects a
specific protocol I have refined, published, and taught to other
surgeons nationally.

Most patients after a traditional tummy tuck describe significant pain
because the abdominal wall is under tension and narcotics are the
primary pain management tool. My approach is different on both counts.

Progressive tension sutures offload incision tension, which reduces the
primary source of post-operative discomfort. Combined with Exparel
(liposomal bupivacaine) and Journavx for multimodal pain control, the
vast majority of my patients describe pressure or tightness — not pain
— in the days following surgery. Many require minimal or no narcotic
medication.

## Surgical technique, step by step

1. **Anatomical evaluation.** Skin laxity, muscle separation, fascial
   integrity, fat distribution — assessed to determine the precise
   surgical plan.
2. **Anesthesia and incision.** General anesthesia. Low, discreet
   bikini-line incision, placed for maximum concealment.
3. **Muscle repair and contouring.** Midline muscles precisely
   reapproximated. Liposuction is integrated when appropriate.
4. **Progressive tension suturing.** Internal sutures secure the
   abdominal flap to the underlying fascia at multiple points across
   the wall — eliminating dead space, redistributing tension. This is
   not a minor technical variation. It changes the entire mechanical
   environment of the healing tissue.
5. **Closure.** Closed in layers with careful attention to tension
   distribution. Compression garment applied.

## Recovery

- **First night.** Dedicated aftercare facility, monitored closely.
- **Day 1.** Return to the office for evaluation and hyperbaric oxygen
  therapy — a structured component of the recovery protocol that
  supports tissue healing and reduces inflammation.
- **First 2 weeks.** Swelling improves progressively. Most patients are
  standing upright and moving more comfortably than expected.
- **6–8 weeks.** Abdominal contour becomes more defined. Most patients
  return to structured exercise.
- **3–6 months.** Progressive contour refinement continues. External
  healing moves faster than internal healing — structural tissue
  maturation continues for months beyond what is visible.

No drains to manage. No drain sites to care for. No drain-related
discomfort. That alone changes the experience of the first week.

## When the tummy tuck is the foundation

The abdomen is not an isolated structure. It is the structural anchor
of the entire torso — and how it is reconstructed affects the
proportional relationship of every surrounding area.

When combining abdominoplasty with breast surgery, liposuction, or
other body contouring procedures, the operation is planned as a
coordinated architectural sequence — not a list of add-ons.
Waist-to-hip ratio, breast projection, core integrity, and long-term
stability are all considered together.
