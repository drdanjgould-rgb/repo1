# Drainless Tummy Tuck

Abdominoplasty performed without postoperative drains. The technique uses
progressive tension sutures (Baroudi-Pollock and related approaches) to
close the dead space between the abdominal wall and the overlying flap,
which is the same space drains were originally designed to manage.

## What it addresses

- Skin laxity and pannus of the lower and mid abdomen
- Stretched abdominal wall musculature (diastasis recti)
- Stretch-marked skin in the area to be excised (within the resected
  segment)
- Contour irregularity from prior pregnancies or significant weight loss

It does not address fat outside the operative field — flanks, hips, and
back are addressed with separate liposuction if appropriate.

## Drainless vs. standard tummy tuck

| Topic                      | Standard                  | Drainless                             |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Postop drain management    | Yes (typically 1–2 weeks) | No                                    |
| Operative time             | Shorter                   | Longer (additional internal suturing) |
| Seroma rate                | Procedure-dependent       | Generally low, technique-dependent    |
| Recovery activity timeline | Comparable                | Comparable                            |

Drainless is a technique choice, not a separate operation. The
appropriate choice for a given patient is decided at consultation based
on anatomy and history.

## Typical recovery

- **Week 1**: most restrictive period; ambulating for circulation but
  otherwise resting. Sleep slightly flexed at the waist. No lifting
  beyond a few pounds.
- **Weeks 2–6**: progressive return to walking, then light activity.
  No high-impact exercise.
- **Week 6+**: gradual return to full exercise as cleared by the surgeon.
- **Months 3–12**: scar maturation; final contour settles as residual
  swelling resolves.

Compression garments are typically worn for several weeks; specifics are
provided at the pre-op visit.

## Candidacy

Assessed at consultation. Common considerations:

- Stable weight (significant weight loss or planned pregnancy are
  conversations to have first)
- Medical clearance for surgery
- Pannus and skin laxity sufficient to warrant resection
- Diastasis recti, if present, can be repaired simultaneously
- Smoking status (cessation period required before surgery)

## Companion procedures

Frequently combined with one or more of:

- Liposuction of the flanks, hips, or upper back
- Mons reduction or lift
- Muscle plication (included in most cases when diastasis is present)
- Mommy makeover combinations (breast procedure during the same surgery)
