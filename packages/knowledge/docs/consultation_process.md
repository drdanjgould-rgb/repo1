# Consultation Process

A consultation at Gould Aesthetics is the starting point for any
procedural conversation. It is the only setting where specific
recommendations, candidacy decisions, and pricing are discussed.

## What a consultation includes

- 60 to 90 minutes with Dr. Gould.
- Discussion of goals, medical history, prior surgeries, current
  medications and supplements, allergies, and any prior aesthetic
  procedures.
- Focused physical examination relevant to the area of interest.
- Three-dimensional volumetric imaging where appropriate, reviewed
  with the patient in real time. Simulated outcomes are generated and
  shared for home review.
- Discussion of realistic options. Surgical and non-surgical
  recommendations are both possible outcomes — not every patient is
  steered toward surgery.
- Surgeon's stated recommendation, including rationale. If a non-surgical
  approach is appropriate, that is recommended.
- Surgical and recovery logistics: anesthesia, OR location, expected
  timeline, follow-up cadence.
- Pricing discussion specific to the recommended plan. **Pricing is
  provided in person after examination, never in advance over DM,
  email, or phone.**
- Hyperbaric oxygen pre-authorization initiated at booking.

## How Dr. Gould opens

Before going deep on the surgical plan, patients usually have two or
three burning questions on the tip of their tongue. Those get
surfaced first — that way the patient can focus on what is said
afterwards. The structured walk-through then covers, in order: the
surgery itself, the downtime, the medications, and safety and
expectations for outcomes.

## How recommendations are framed

Dr. Gould states his recommendation explicitly. When two options have
been discussed, he says which he would do — and why — for that
specific patient's anatomy and goals.

When a recommendation is given, the language is often: _if you were
my sister, I would tell you to do X._

When a procedure is not appropriate, that is also stated explicitly.
Fat transfer is positioned, when discussed, as not a lesser option
because of safety — it is a lesser option for specific anatomy and
goals. The framing is _I want you to be happy, not just okay._

## What to bring

- Government-issued photo ID
- Current medication list, including supplements
- Any prior operative reports, imaging, or pathology relevant to the
  consult
- Photo references if helpful — patients sometimes find it easier to
  show than describe
- Questions written down

## Virtual consults

Available for out-of-area patients. A virtual visit follows the same
structure, with examination limited to what photos and video convey.
An in-person visit is still required before any surgical decision is
finalized.

## Surgical timing and candidacy

When a patient has had prior facial surgery elsewhere, **a minimum of
twelve months** of healing is required before secondary deep plane
surgery can be safely undertaken. Internal soft tissue healing
continues well beyond external wound closure; operating within an
incompletely healed deep tissue environment increases risk and
reduces the likelihood of an optimal outcome.

This applies even when external scars look fully resolved. Operating
before twelve months means operating in an environment that has not
finished organizing itself — scar tissue is still remodeling, tissue
planes are still in flux. Working against the biology instead of with
it.

## After the consult

- A written summary is sent. It includes the surgical or non-surgical
  recommendation, the proposed plan, and pricing for the recommended
  plan.
- 3D imaging results are transmitted for home review.
- Scheduling, deposit, pre-op visit, and surgery date are coordinated
  by the surgical scheduler.
- Hyperbaric oxygen therapy is pre-authorized.
- Compounded topical prescription is issued where appropriate.
- Follow-up is included for the post-op period and beyond at no
  additional cost.

## Pre-operative appointment

A separate visit before surgery covers: final surgical plan
confirmation, pre-op instructions, final consent discussion, last
patient questions, anesthesia discussion. The phone call also walks
through the four-concept framework one more time.

## Communication style: educational first, decisions in person

Patients receive a structured summary email written in Dr. Gould's
voice after the visit. The summary covers what was discussed in plain
language, restates limitations and non-guarantees in warm phrasing,
and ends with a clear next step. The disclaimer at the bottom
reinforces that no outcome was guaranteed.

This is a deliberate clinical practice: it ensures the patient has
written reference of expectations set and limitations discussed, and
that there is a contemporaneous record of what was promised — and what
was not.

## What we do not say in DM

A specific recommendation, candidacy decision, or price for an
individual patient before a consultation. Those are surgical decisions
that require an examination by Dr. Gould.
