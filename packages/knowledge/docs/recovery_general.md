# General Recovery Principles

Applies broadly to surgical procedures performed at Gould Plastic Surgery.
Procedure-specific instructions take precedence and are provided at the
pre-op visit.

## Phases of recovery

- **Phase 1 — Days 0 through 7.** Acute swelling and bruising peak around
  day 3. Activity is restricted; ambulation for circulation is
  encouraged. Pain is most pronounced during this window and managed per
  the post-op medication plan. Sleep position is typically modified
  (head elevated for facial procedures; slightly flexed at the waist
  for abdominal procedures).
- **Phase 2 — Weeks 2 through 6.** Most visible bruising resolves;
  patients gradually return to non-physical work and social activities.
  Exercise and lifting are reintroduced incrementally per surgeon
  guidance. Garments and dressings transition to lighter forms.
- **Phase 3 — Months 3 through 12.** Residual edema and firmness
  continue to soften. Scars mature, lightening and softening over the
  full year. Final contour settles during this period.

## Routine instructions

Procedure-specific, but common themes:

- Strict adherence to lifting and exercise restrictions during Phase 1.
- Avoid nicotine in any form for the period specified by the surgeon
  before and after surgery.
- Avoid blood-thinning medications and supplements per the pre-op
  instructions.
- Wear compression garments as directed.
- Sleep position as instructed.
- Keep incisions clean and dry; specific wound-care instructions are
  procedure-specific.

## What is normal in the early window

- Bruising and asymmetric swelling that follows gravity (worse on
  one side depending on sleep position)
- Tightness, numbness, or tingling in operative areas
- Itching as healing progresses
- Lumps or firmness along incision lines that soften with time
- Mood changes during the first 1–2 weeks

These typically resolve and do not require urgent contact.

## When to contact the office

Reach out to the practice during business hours for routine concerns.
Use the **after-hours line** or, in an emergency, **call 911** for any of:

- Bleeding that soaks through dressings or doesn't stop with gentle
  pressure
- Sudden, severe asymmetric swelling
- Fever above 101°F
- Pain that is worsening rather than improving over time
- Wound separation, drainage that is foul-smelling or thick
- Shortness of breath, chest pain, calf pain or swelling
- Sudden vision changes after facial procedures
- Numbness that is new or worsening

The chatbot does not provide medical advice. Any symptom concern is
routed to a team member who calls the patient back.

## What we do not say in DM

Procedure-specific recovery timelines for the individual patient,
medication adjustments, or any clinical decision. Those happen with the
clinical team.
