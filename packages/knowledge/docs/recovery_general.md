# General Recovery Principles

Procedure-specific instructions take precedence and are provided at the
pre-op visit. This is the general scaffolding Dr. Gould walks patients
through.

## The four-concept framework

Every pre-op review covers four things in this order:

1. **The surgery itself** — what is being done, anatomically.
2. **The downtime** — what each phase looks like.
3. **The medications** — what is given and why.
4. **Safety and expectations for outcomes.**

Before going deep on any of them, patients usually have two or three
burning questions on the tip of the tongue. Those get answered first.
Then we dive deep.

## Phases of recovery: day zero through two years

- **Day 0.** Immediate post-anesthesia recovery. Patient goes to a
  dedicated aftercare facility where nurses give medications for sleep,
  nausea, and discomfort. Dr. Gould visits in person — that conversation
  is the one the patient will remember.
- **Day 1.** Head wrap or dressings come down in the office.
  Hyperbaric oxygen therapy initiated. When skin is elevated from its
  blood supply, or when fat is transferred from one body region to
  another, the tissue cries out for oxygen by secreting inflammatory
  factors. HBOT delivers that oxygen: inflammation and swelling go
  down, skin healing improves, fat survival improves, secondary
  metabolites clear faster, the patient feels more awake.
- **Days 3–5.** Oral medications completing. Walking around the house.
  Things moving in the right direction.
- **~Day 7.** Patient is at their best surgically — sutures may come
  out, swelling has peaked and is receding. **Lowest emotionally** —
  withdrawal from narcotics, anesthesia, adrenaline, and cortisol. This
  is when patients look in the mirror and think _what was I thinking_.
  This is expected. It is normal. It is part of the process.
- **Day 10.** Out of the inflammatory window. Comfortable being around
  people.
- **Two weeks.** Comfortable in a grocery store — no one is looking
  twice. Upright cardio cleared (bike, Stairmaster, elliptical) at low
  speed. Lymphatic massage typically initiated.
- **Weeks 3–4.** Bruising resolved. Cheek and jawline swelling
  continues but the patient's face is the patient's face — others do
  not notice unless they have a magnifying glass.
- **Week 6.** Pool or ocean cleared. Fully cleared for the gym at full
  intensity. Compliments start arriving. People say things like _have
  you lost weight_ and _you look so well rested_. CO2 laser at 6 weeks
  – 3 months further refines the result.
- **3 months.** Ready for high-definition photography or a wedding.
  100% healed on the outside; ~50% on the inside. Additional injections,
  massage, or laser can fine-tune.
- **6 months.** ~60% internal healing.
- **1 year.** ~75–80%.
- **2 years.** Fully healed on the inside.

It is not just surgery and see you later. There is a lot the team does
afterwards to continue to modulate healing and improve the result.

## Routine instructions (procedure-dependent specifics)

- Strict adherence to lifting and exercise restrictions in Phase 1.
- Avoid nicotine in any form for the period specified.
- Avoid blood-thinning medications and supplements per pre-op
  instructions.
- GLP-1 medications: hold approximately 2 weeks before and 4 weeks
  after, to avoid suppressing the protein and energy intake the body
  needs for early healing.
- Compression garments worn as directed.
- Sleep position as instructed (head elevated for facial; slightly
  flexed at the waist for abdominal).
- Wound care per procedure.
- Foods to avoid in the first week of deep-neck work: sour, spicy,
  very salty, or very sweet — these trigger inflammation in the deep
  neck where the salivary glands are. Eat protein-dense soft foods.

## Anti-nausea protocol

Most common complaint after this surgery is a headache and a sore
throat. Both are managed.

For nausea specifically: oral Emend pre-op, IV steroid intraop,
phenergan and zofran around the clock post-op, scopolamine patch
intraop. In the last six years, two nauseous patients — both
specifically because protocol was deviated from (one declined the
Emend; one took too much narcotic).

## What is normal in the early window

- Bruising and asymmetric swelling that follows gravity
- Tightness, numbness, or tingling in operative areas
- Itching as healing progresses
- Lumps or firmness along incision lines that soften with time
- Mood changes during the first 1–2 weeks, especially around day 7

These resolve and do not require urgent contact.

## When to contact the office — the red-flag list

Reach out to the practice during business hours for routine concerns.
Use the **after-hours line** or, in an emergency, **call 911**, for
any of:

- Bleeding that soaks through dressings or doesn't stop with gentle
  pressure
- Sudden, severe asymmetric swelling
- Fever above 101°F
- Pain that is worsening rather than improving over time
- Wound separation, drainage that is foul-smelling or thick
- Shortness of breath, chest pain, calf pain or swelling
- Sudden vision changes after facial procedures
- Numbness that is new or worsening

The chatbot does not provide medical advice. Any symptom concern is
routed to a team member who calls the patient back.

## Safety order

1. Safety.
2. How good you look.
3. Quality of the experience.

If safety would require accepting a slightly less-good aesthetic
result, that trade is taken — every time. The practice is willing to
accept going from a 95 to a 96 instead of going for a 96 if it risks
the chance of a 0.

## What we do not say in DM

Procedure-specific recovery timelines for the individual patient,
medication adjustments, or any clinical decision. Those happen with
the clinical team.
