# Deep Plane Facelift

A surgical procedure performed at Gould Plastic Surgery to address descent
of the midface, jowls, and upper neck. The technique releases facial
ligaments at the deep plane and repositions the underlying soft tissues
as a single unit, rather than tightening skin over a fixed deeper layer.

## What it addresses

- Loss of definition along the jawline and the appearance of jowls
- Descent of the midface and cheek fat compartments
- Laxity along the upper neck and submandibular region
- Deepening of the nasolabial folds and labiomandibular grooves
- Loss of contour at the cervicomental angle when combined with deep neck work

## How it differs from other facelift techniques

- **SMAS facelift**: dissection is at a more superficial layer (the SMAS
  flap is lifted off the underlying tissues). Effects are real but
  shorter-lived in patients with significant deep-plane descent.
- **Mini-lift / short-scar lift**: smaller incisions, more limited
  dissection, addresses early jowl/jawline laxity. Not appropriate for
  significant midface or neck descent.
- **Deep plane**: dissection is below the SMAS, releasing named facial
  ligaments (zygomatic, masseteric cutaneous). The midface, cheek, and
  jowl move as a structural unit. Generally longer-lasting in patients
  who are appropriate candidates.

Technique selection depends on anatomy and goals — not all patients are
deep-plane candidates.

## Typical recovery

- **Days 0–7**: peak swelling and bruising; restricted activity; sleep
  with head elevated. Drains may be used; care instructions provided.
- **Weeks 2–4**: visible bruising substantially resolved. Most patients
  return to non-physical work and social activities within 2–3 weeks.
- **Weeks 4–12**: residual firmness and edema continue to soften.
- **Months 6–12**: final settling of contour. Scars continue to mature
  for up to a year.

Wound-care, activity, and sleep instructions are procedure-specific and
provided at the pre-op visit.

## Companion procedures

Often performed together with one or more of:

- Deep neck work (digastric muscle plication, subplatysmal fat reduction,
  submandibular gland reduction when appropriate)
- Upper or lower blepharoplasty
- Fat transfer to midface, temples, or tear troughs
- Brow lift
- Skin resurfacing (laser, peel)

The recommendation is based on anatomy at the consult, not on a menu.

## Candidacy

Candidacy is assessed at consultation. Considerations include skin
quality, underlying skeletal structure, soft-tissue volume distribution,
medical history, and patient goals. Some patients are better served by
non-surgical options (energy-based skin tightening, fillers) or by a
smaller-scope procedure.
